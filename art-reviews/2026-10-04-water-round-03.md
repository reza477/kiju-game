## Improvement and remaining ranked findings

1. **Constrain the remaining joined bright features, especially at close range.** Compared with round 2, `03-sun-facing.jpg` and `04-sun-facing-later.jpg` show more dark water separating highlights. This is a real benefit. Nevertheless `05-sun-close.jpg` and the later phase in `10-shore-return.jpg` still contain broad connected white/pale areas. The original white-carpet artifact is reduced across the update but not consistently resolved across zooms and phases. A final bounded refinement should shorten or interrupt these strongest connected features while leaving the lower-energy water response readable. Their brightest cores need not disappear. This is not a request to dim all water or change the sun/world lighting.

2. **Break the residual far-channel rhythm with clearer quiet gaps.** The pattern is more irregular than round 1 but still tends toward many similarly sized short transverse crests, especially above the carrier in the reverse and close views. Local areas of calmer water would make the active areas feel less like a repeating texture. This is an ordinary polish limitation, not a newly identified runtime failure. Avoid adding fine noisy sparkle to disguise the larger rhythm.

3. **Protect normal-zoom readability.** At the ordinary camera, the river is recognizably active without needing a close inspection. Its more restrained colour and moving detail should survive the final adjustment. A uniformly dull or nearly static surface would be a regression. The useful remaining scope is the distribution of crests and strong glints, not new water systems, geometry or environmental content.

The portrait views preserve this distinction: ordinary water remains readable in `07-portrait-normal.jpg`; the sun-facing strip in `08-portrait-sun.jpg` contains separated darker areas but still has strongly bright joined patches. No claim is made that sustained mobile shimmer or aliasing has been measured.
