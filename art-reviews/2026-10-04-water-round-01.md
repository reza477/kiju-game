## Ranked water-only findings

1. **Break up the repeated transverse wave rows.** At ordinary normal/reverse zoom, too many similarly spaced, similarly oriented crests span most of the channel. The underlying rhythm remains legible even though individual edges contain noise. See `03-city-normal-later.jpg` and `04-city-reverse.jpg`. Vary crest length, spacing, strength and continuity; preserve some calmer gaps. Adding finer noise alone would not solve the larger repeated pattern. This can be addressed within the water material without moving the river or its banks.

2. **Moderate and fragment the sun-facing glare.** In `06-sun-facing-later.jpg` and `07-sun-facing-close.jpg`, a large part of the near channel becomes a bright white ladder-like carpet. The water's underlying colour and calmer areas are overwhelmed, and the repeated rhythm becomes more obvious. The same problem survives portrait emulation in `10-portrait-emulation-sun.jpg`. Retain glints and directional light response, but reduce the extent of merged white coverage and preserve visible water between highlights. Do not compensate by changing the world's lighting or flattening all reflection.

3. **Keep the improved motion readable while refining it.** The broad changing structure is a real benefit. An overcorrection that makes the river uniformly dark or almost static at normal zoom would lose that benefit. The useful target is less regular, less glaring movement, not uniformly weaker movement. The near and far channel should retain a related appearance as the camera orbits.

These recommendations concern the water surface only. They do not request new gameplay, wakes, buoyancy, changed river geometry, different shores, foliage work, camera redesign or an environment rewrite.

At portrait size the bright pattern is especially dominant in the narrow visible river strip. Sparkle severity over sustained phone motion was not measured and is not asserted as a separately confirmed defect. QA's profile/performance evidence remains separate.
